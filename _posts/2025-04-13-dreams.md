*Dreams are worth pursuing*

I was going to wait to post this, but Rory McIlroy just had his dream come true. It's appropriate that I talk about mine. He is now a Masters champion, the 6th player ever to complete the career Grand Slam. It made me emotional, not just because of all that he has gone through, but it was a nice parallel to what happened to me this month.

It was 10:34am. I was playing Nintendo Switch with my son because he is on Spring break and I had some vacation time to burn. The phone is ringing, and I immediately recognize the number. It was HR from the company that I just interviewed with less than 24 hours prior. 

**They were offering me the job.** 

It's April Fool's Day. Surely this cannot be real. Not that someone would play a sick joke and pretend to make my childhood dream come true, but... this day of all days?

Yes, in fact. On April 1st, 2025, I received a phone call that is the culmination of four years of hard work, sacrifice, and a whole lot of doubt. 

I am now *officially* an aerospace engineer.

I thought I picked one of the worst times in history to try to become an engineer in the space industry. NASA and Blue Origin both cut a lot of jobs, there is a lot of uncertainty on if the focus will shift from the Moon to Mars, and a few of my leads dried up due to contracts not getting renewed. Why would a company take a chance on me, with no experience in aerospace, when there is a huge influx of people with the resumes to fill that job? The doubts and apprehensions, and not to mention the continuous feeling of inadequacy, were almost strong enough for me to end the search and let the dream die. There were more than a few times that I wanted to give up. It was too easy for me to say that returning to school was regrettable and a huge waste. I would tell myself that the time that I spent studying or working late on the motorcycle were for naught.

My only slight glimmer of hope was that Cleveland and NASA GRC roles would be less enticing to those who were just laid off in much warmer climates. Cleveland has always been at the top of the list for me, even before I married someone from NE Ohio who loved the idea of moving closer to family. I also knew that if, and that ended up being a huge if, I could get an interview that I would have a good chance of getting the job. This was true in July 2024, but then the job had to be pulled because of issues with the contract. I got to roughly 40 applications with only one interview, far fewer than many that are looking for roles, but I was being very specific with the locations and the job descriptions to give myself the best chance. That also means that I probably changed my resume about that many times to match. I also wrote cover letters, first very boring and professional, then eventually more personal and authentic. 

Maybe that ended up being the difference.

The one that got through was the 7th application at the same company. In retrospect, some attempts were more ambitious than others, but I really didn't have a good gauge on how my experience would translate. I asked around and got some advice, and I think it did end up being pretty accurate. I did a majority of my job search through LinkedIn, and saw a job listing that seemed like it would be one of my best chances. It aligned well with my senior design project and technical electives, so I knew that I could hit a good chunk of the keywords on my resume. The email for the phone screen came two weeks after applying, and then I had an in-person interview scheduled a week after that call. I had the option to do the interview virtually, but I had already taken the week off to align with my son's Spring break. I also figured that it showed how much I wanted the job, that I would drive up from Columbus instead of taking the easy option. I drove up the night before to stay with the in laws, which was the short term plan should I get a job in the area anyway. The interview went as well as I could have hoped, but I have always been confident in my interviewing skills. I was told that they were conducting more interviews that week and that I would hear about the decision in a week or so.

20 hours later, I got the call. 

The late nights studying. The long hours working on campus. Jumping from class into a meeting then onto another class.

It was all worth it. 

I am still waiting for my first day in the position as I write this, but I am full of so much excitement and feel so validated that I accomplished my goals so completely. I get to work at NASA (albeit as a contractor) on a lunar mission at NASA Glenn. It means that I don't have to regret going back to school and don't have to think about the feelings of failure and rejection when each application would result in nothing. I instead get to share with others that although it may not be as perfect of a process as someone would hope, the fight for a goal can be fruitful. I also want to emphasize that my feelings of regret about all of this are really just me being melodramatic. I gained way more than just a degree and a chance to be a rocket scientist through this. I know that it is easy for me to say that now that I got the result that I wanted, but I also know how much I enjoyed the process, thrived in the journey. Throughout all of this, I can come to one conclusion.

Dreams are worth pursuing.
